# Research Methodology

UX research methodology frameworks based on the UK Government Design Manual (GDS) and industry best practices.

## When to Use This Skill

- Planning a research study from scratch
- Deciding which research method to use
- Understanding the 4 phases of research: Discovery, Alpha, Beta, Live
- Designing a research strategy aligned with product lifecycle

## The 4 Phases of Research

### 1. DISCOVERY — "What is the problem?"

**Goal**: Understand the problem space, user needs, and context.

**Methods**:
- Contextual inquiry (observe users in their environment)
- Ethnography (deep immersion in user context)
- Stakeholder interviews (understand business constraints)
- Diary studies (longitudinal behavior tracking)
- Guerrilla research (quick, informal feedback)

**Outputs**: User needs, problem definitions, research questions, initial personas.

**Duration**: 2-6 weeks depending on complexity.

### 2. ALPHA — "Are we solving the right problem?"

**Goal**: Test early concepts and prototypes with users.

**Methods**:
- Usability testing (moderated, think-aloud)
- Concept testing (validate value propositions)
- Card sorting (information architecture)
- Tree testing (navigation validation)
- Content testing (clarity of language)

**Outputs**: Validated concepts, usability issues, iteration priorities.

**Duration**: 2-4 weeks per iteration.

### 3. BETA — "Does our solution work?"

**Goal**: Refine the MVP with real users in realistic conditions.

**Methods**:
- Usability testing (moderated and unmoderated)
- A/B testing (compare design variations)
- Accessibility testing (WCAG compliance)
- Performance testing (speed, reliability impact on UX)

**Outputs**: Refined MVP, accessibility compliance, performance benchmarks.

**Duration**: 4-8 weeks.

### 4. LIVE — "Is it getting better?"

**Goal**: Continuous optimization based on real user behavior.

**Methods**:
- Analytics review (behavioral data patterns)
- Satisfaction surveys (NPS, CSAT, SUS)
- Ongoing usability testing (spot-check new features)
- Feedback analysis (support tickets, reviews, social media)
- A/B testing (incremental improvements)

**Outputs**: Optimization backlog, trend reports, satisfaction metrics.

**Duration**: Ongoing, integrated into every sprint.

## Method Selection Framework

| What You Need to Know | Best Method | Sample Size |
|----------------------|-------------|-------------|
| User needs and context | Contextual inquiry, ethnography | 5-8 per segment |
| Task completion issues | Usability testing | 5 per round |
| Information architecture | Card sorting, tree testing | 20-30 |
| Content clarity | Content testing | 5-8 |
| Design preference | A/B testing | 100+ per variant |
| Satisfaction level | Surveys (NPS, CSAT, SUS) | 50+ |
| Longitudinal behavior | Diary studies | 10-15 |
| Quick validation | Guerrilla research | 5-10 |

## Key Principles

1. **Behavior > Opinion**: What users do matters more than what they say.
2. **Context is King**: Understanding the environment is as important as understanding the user.
3. **5 Users = 85% of Problems**: With just 5 users you discover most critical issues.
4. **Triangulation**: Cross-validate findings across multiple methods and sources.
5. **Analysis Time = Research Time**: Reserve equal time for analysis as for data collection.

## Evidence Standards

Every finding must include:
- **What**: The specific observation or data point
- **Evidence**: Direct quote, screenshot, or metric
- **Source**: Where it came from (method, participant, date)
- **Severity**: Critical / Major / Minor / Cosmetic
- **Recommendation**: Specific, actionable next step
